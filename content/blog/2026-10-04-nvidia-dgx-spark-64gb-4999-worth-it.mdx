NVIDIA is betting that the future of AI sits on your desk, not in someone else's data center. This week it backed that bet with cheaper hardware: a **64GB DGX Spark** desktop starting at **$4,999**, on sale Friday, October 23.

![Flat illustration of a compact desktop AI computer box on a desk with glowing circuit traces and floating chat bubbles](/blog-images/nvidia-dgx-spark-64gb-4999-worth-it-hero.webp)

## What the 64GB DGX Spark is

It is a small desktop box built around NVIDIA's GB10 Grace Blackwell superchip with **64GB of unified memory** — one pool shared by the CPU and the GPU. Unified memory matters because it removes the usual VRAM limit: a big model no longer has to squeeze into a separate, smaller graphics-card memory.

The 64GB box keeps the same chip, DGX OS, and NVIDIA AI software stack as the 128GB model. NVIDIA says a single unit runs models with **up to 100 billion parameters** entirely on the device. Out of the box it supports Ollama, vLLM, llama.cpp, LM Studio, and PyTorch with CUDA, and NVIDIA names models like Gemma4 26B, Qwen3.8-27B, Meta Muse Glimmer, and Nemotron3.5 Lightning.

Two 64GB units can be linked directly over a ConnectX-7 network cable to pool **128GB of unified memory**, reaching models up to 200 billion parameters. NVIDIA's own internal test on Qwen 3.8 27B showed the pair delivering up to **1.7x the performance** of a single 128GB system. A Sync Model Launcher arrives at the end of the month, promising one-click Qwen3.8 27B downloads and OpenCode configuration from a browser.

## The honest catch

First, the price trend is the wrong way around. The 128GB Founders Edition launched at $3,999, rose to $4,699 in February, and now lists at **$6,950** — NVIDIA cites memory supply constraints. The new 64GB box starts at $4,999, which is $1,000 more than the 128GB model originally cost.

Second, capacity decides what runs. Roughly 8GB of the 64GB goes to the system, leaving about **56GB for model weights and KV cache**. That means gpt-oss 120B, which needs around 65GB, simply does not fit. And for pure inference per dollar, independent analysis points at 128GB AMD Strix Halo boxes at $2,959–$3,649 as the value pick. If you are shopping for a local-AI machine, compare carefully in [/compare/hardware](/compare/hardware) before buying anything.

## The part that's free: faster software

Here is the news that costs you nothing. NVIDIA's updated llama.cpp backend delivers **up to 1.9x higher throughput on a GeForce RTX 5090**, and vLLM shows **up to 1.4x** gains on two-DGX-Spark clusters. Because these are engine-level optimizations, they reach you through apps like Ollama and LM Studio — an update, not a purchase.

![Bar chart of NVIDIA's claimed local-inference speedups: llama.cpp 1.9x on RTX 5090, vLLM 1.4x on two Sparks, two clustered 64GB Sparks 1.7x, PAIR about 2x across three PCs](/blog-images/nvidia-dgx-spark-64gb-4999-worth-it-fig1.webp)

*All four figures are NVIDIA's own claimed numbers, not independent tests — treat them as the ceiling, not a promise for your setup.*

## PAIR: your other PCs can help

The third announcement is a free, open-source beta called **PAIR** (Personal AI Router). It discovers compatible PCs on your local network and spreads AI inference work across them. Important detail: it does not fuse several GPUs into one bigger machine. It distributes **independent inference requests** across devices — which is exactly what helps when an AI agent splits a job into parallel sub-tasks.

It works with Ollama and LM Studio on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and supports GeForce RTX 20-series cards and newer, Apple M4 chips and later, and DGX Spark. In NVIDIA's test, five sub-agents working simultaneously took about 18 minutes on one device but finished in under 9 minutes across three machines.

This lands alongside the wider October hardware push: RTX Spark Windows PCs arriving this month from Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft, and MSI — compact machines built around local AI agents, creators, and gaming on one architecture.

## The takeaway

Do not start by buying. If you already own an NVIDIA RTX card, the free move is updating your stack — llama.cpp, Ollama, or LM Studio — and taking the software speedup. If your home has two or three capable PCs, PAIR can put them to work together at no cost.

The 64GB DGX Spark is a real product for a real audience: people who want a dedicated CUDA dev box for models up to about 100B parameters, with a two-box path to 128GB. Just know what you are buying — and what it cannot hold — before you spend $5,000. Run your workload through [/check](/check) first.
