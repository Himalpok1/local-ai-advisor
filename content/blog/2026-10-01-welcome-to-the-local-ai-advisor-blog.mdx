Running AI on your own computer has gone from a hobby for researchers to something you can set up in an afternoon. The news moves fast, though, and most of it is written for people who already know the jargon. This blog is for everyone else.

## What you'll find here

One or two short posts most days, each answering a simple question: **does this change what I can do with AI on my own machine?**

- **New open models.** When a lab releases a model whose weights anyone can download, we explain what it is good at and what kind of computer it needs.
- **Apps and tools.** Free apps such as [Ollama](https://ollama.com) and [LM Studio](https://lmstudio.ai) make local AI point-and-click. When they add something useful, we show you how to use it.
- **Hardware.** Memory matters more than almost anything else for local AI. We cover new laptops, desktops and graphics cards in terms of what they let you run.
- **Plain explanations.** Words like *quantization* and *context window* get a sentence of explanation the first time they appear, with a link to our [free course](/learn) if you want more.

## How we keep it honest

Local AI is genuinely useful, and it also has real limits. A model running on a laptop is usually smaller than the ones behind the biggest cloud chatbots, and it can be slower. We will say so when that matters.

Every post follows the same rules:

1. **Written for beginners.** If a sentence needs a glossary, we rewrite the sentence.
2. **No hype.** "Runs on a laptop" means comfortably, at a speed you would actually use, not "technically loads after ten minutes".
3. **Every factual claim has a source.** You'll find them in a *Sources* section at the end of each post.

## Who writes it

Posts are researched and drafted by Ray, an AI assistant working for Himal, who runs this site. Using AI to write about AI is fitting, but it is also why the rules above exist: Ray cites sources so you can check the facts yourself, and the speed and memory estimates come from the same [published methodology](/methodology) as the rest of the site.
