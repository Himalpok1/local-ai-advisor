Image models used to hand you one of two bills: an API charge for every picture, or a 24GB graphics card on your desk. On October 15, Black Forest Labs released **FLUX 3 Image Klein**, a 5.6-billion-parameter open-weight image model whose INT8 build is designed to run on consumer GPUs with as little as **8GB of VRAM**. That puts near-frontier image generation and editing on the machine a gaming laptop already has.

![Flat illustration of a graphics card on a desk generating an image on a nearby monitor](/blog-images/flux-3-image-klein-8gb-gpu-local-hero.webp)

## What it is

FLUX 3 Image Klein is the compact open-weight member of Black Forest Labs' FLUX 3 family. One checkpoint covers three jobs that used to mean three different tools:

- **Text-to-image generation**, with native output up to **4K** resolution.
- **Image editing** — BFL's claim is that unedited regions are preserved exactly, not approximately repainted.
- **Multi-reference generation** with up to **10 reference images** — keep a character or a product consistent across images without retraining.

It also supports **bounding-box control**: you draw a box to say where an element goes, or to confine an edit to one region. Anyone who has fought a model that kept moving a logo knows why that matters more than another benchmark point.

The release ships two checkpoints: a **base model** for fine-tuning and research, and a **step-distilled model** for fast generation. NVIDIA helped build quantized versions in **FP8, INT8 and NVFP4** alongside it.

![Diagram showing how bounding-box editing confines a change to the drawn rectangle while the rest of the image stays unchanged](/blog-images/flux-3-image-klein-8gb-gpu-local-fig2.webp)

## The memory math: what fits on your GPU

BFL reports the generator weights take **11.2 GB in BF16** and **5.6 GB in FP8**. The INT8 build is the one aimed at consumer cards: BFL says it runs through ComfyUI or Diffusers on GeForce RTX cards with 8GB of VRAM.

One honest caveat before you download. The 5.6B figure covers the **image generator only**, not the text encoder that reads your prompt. Hugging Face's own tests found the full pipeline used roughly **16 GB with FP8 weights and about 21 GB in BF16** on data-center GPUs. Getting it onto an 8GB card means using the INT8 build plus memory-saving tricks — Hugging Face suggests running the text encoder separately, for example.

So the fair version of the claim: 8GB is the floor with the INT8 build and careful setup, not plug-and-play on default settings. A card with 12–16GB will have a much easier time.

## How fast it is

All speed numbers below are vendor-reported, measured with the distilled model at 16 steps on a 1-megapixel image:

![Bar chart of vendor-reported generation times: under 0.6s on GB300, about 2s on H100, about 4s on RTX 5090, under 9s on RTX 4070](/blog-images/flux-3-image-klein-8gb-gpu-local-fig1.webp)

Under 9 seconds per image on an RTX 4070 is a usable local pace — you can iterate on a prompt without a queue or a meter running. BFL's Hugging Face Diffusers announcement separately measured about 2 seconds for a 1024×1024 image on an H100 with the distilled checkpoint.

## The license: read the revenue line

The weights ship under the **FLUX Kommunity License**: free for commercial use by individuals and businesses with **annual revenue under $5 million**, plus research and non-commercial use. Companies above that line need a paid self-hosted license or must use the API.

That is more permissive than many image-model licenses, but note one wrinkle reported in launch coverage: BFL's model page also says running Klein in a product needs a commercial license. Check the model card terms before you ship anything customer-facing.

## API or your own machine?

If you'd rather not run it, the BFL API serves the distilled model at **$0.009 per 1K-resolution image** — about 110 images for a dollar. It is also available in BFL's Playground and on Hugging Face Spaces.

For tinkerers, LoRA fine-tuning works from day one through Diffusers, Ostris's AI Toolkit, and NVIDIA's NeMo AutoModel — and the base checkpoint exists specifically for that. Community fine-tunes usually land within weeks of a launch like this.

## What I can't confirm yet

Independent tests had not landed at writing time. The quality claims are BFL's own: Klein scores the **highest Elo ratings among open models** in their internal testing, beating some closed models including Ideogram V4.5, and gains **91 Elo points over FLUX.2 klein** (the 9B predecessor) with 38% fewer parameters. Treat those as the vendor's claims until third-party testing exists. Klein is also the second FLUX 3 open release after FLUX 3 Action (a 7B robot-control model), and BFL says more releases are coming.

## Takeaway

Klein is the most practical kind of image-model news for local-AI users: not a bigger model, but a smaller one doing nearly the same job. If your card has 8–16GB of VRAM, an INT8 build running locally in ComfyUI is now the cheapest way to get bounding-box editing, 10-reference composition and near-4K output — no API bill, no files leaving your machine.
